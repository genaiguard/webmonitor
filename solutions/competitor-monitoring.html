Competitor monitoring for small teams

Add a competitor's domain and WebMonitor finds its pricing, changelog, blog and careers pages, checks them on a schedule, and sends you a short brief every Monday of what changed and why it matters.

Start freeHow it works

Competitor monitoring means keeping track of what your competitors publish: price changes, new plans, product launches, positioning changes, new hires and partnerships. Most small teams do it by opening a few tabs when they remember to, which means changes get noticed weeks late or in a sales call. Enterprise competitive intelligence platforms solve this, but are priced for teams with a dedicated analyst. WebMonitor sits in between: it watches the pages where competitors announce things, reads each change with AI, and turns a week of changes into one brief your team will actually read.

What it does

Watchlists that find the pages for you

Enter a domain and WebMonitor reads the competitor's sitemap and homepage links to find its pricing, changelog, blog, careers and integrations pages. Pick the ones you want and they are watched from then on.

A weekly brief, not a stream of alerts

Every Monday each watchlist gets a summary of the past week: what changed on which competitor, in plain sentences, with links to the pages. Quiet weeks get a one-line note. It goes to email and, if you add a webhook, to Slack.

Changes read by AI

Each check compares the page with the last reading and describes the change: a new plan, a raised price, a new integration, a role opened in a new region. Cosmetic edits do not count as changes.

Alerts on the things that cannot wait

Any page can also have its own plain-English rule, such as "tell me if the Pro plan price changes" or "alert me when they post a sales role in Germany", which sends an email the moment it matches.

A free snapshot to start

Before paying, get a one-page snapshot of one competitor: their positioning, current plans and prices, recent moves, hiring and what is worth watching. No credit card.

Priced for small teams

Starter is $29/mo for 25 pages checked every 6 hours. Growth is $79/mo for 100 pages checked hourly. Agency is $149/mo for 250 pages, for consultants watching markets for several clients.

How to set it up

1

Get a snapshot of your main competitor

Enter their domain. The snapshot shows what WebMonitor can read on their site and what it found today.

2

Turn it into a watchlist

Keep the suggested pages (usually pricing, changelog, blog and careers) or add others. Add three to five competitors to one watchlist so the brief compares them.

3

Add rules for urgent changes

Most changes can wait for Monday. For the few that cannot, such as a price cut on a competitor you lose deals to, add a rule and get an email right away.

4

Share the brief

Send the brief to a #competitors Slack channel so sales, product and marketing all see the same thing at the start of the week.

Honest limits

WebMonitor sees what competitors publish on their public websites. It does not see social media behind a login, ads, email campaigns, unannounced plans or anything behind a sign-in. Some sites block automated reading or show different prices by region or after a quiz; the snapshot shows you up front which pages could be read. Pricing pages that only say "contact sales" will show no price changes, because there is nothing published to change.

Frequently asked questions

What is competitor monitoring?

Competitor monitoring is the ongoing tracking of what competitors change in public: pricing, plans, product features, messaging, hiring and announcements. The goal is to hear about a change when it happens instead of weeks later, so pricing, sales and roadmap decisions are made with current information.

Which competitor pages are worth monitoring?

Pricing pages change least often but matter most. Changelogs and release notes show what is being built. Careers pages show where a company is investing, such as a first sales hire in a new country. The blog and newsroom carry launches and partnerships. A watchlist suggests these pages for each competitor automatically.

How is this different from Crayon or Klue?

Crayon and Klue are full competitive intelligence platforms built for teams with a dedicated analyst, with battlecards, CRM integrations and curated news, and they are sold on annual contracts that buyers report at roughly $12,000 to $60,000 a year. WebMonitor covers the part most small teams need, which is knowing what changed on competitor websites each week, for $29 to $149 a month with no contract.

How is this different from a page change detector like Visualping?

Change detectors alert you when a page changes, one page at a time. WebMonitor groups a competitor's pages into a watchlist, reads each change with AI so cosmetic edits are left out, and summarises the week across all your competitors in one brief.

Can I monitor competitor LinkedIn or social posts?

No. LinkedIn, X and Instagram sit behind logins and WebMonitor does not get around them. The same announcements usually appear on the competitor's own blog, newsroom or careers page, which WebMonitor can watch.

Will my competitors know I am monitoring them?

No. WebMonitor reads public pages from its own servers, the same way a visitor would. Nothing in the request identifies you or your company.

Is there a free plan?

You can get one free competitor snapshot with no credit card. Watchlists and the weekly brief are on the paid plans, from $29 a month.
